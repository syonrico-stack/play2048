What is 2048?

2048 is a single-player sliding tile puzzle game that took the internet by storm. The game is played on a simple 4×4 grid. Every move you make slides all the tiles in one direction, and when two tiles with the same number collide, they merge into one tile showing their sum. After each move, a new tile — either a 2 or a 4 — appears in a random empty cell. Your goal is to keep merging tiles until you create the elusive 2048 tile.

The rules take ten seconds to learn, but the strategy takes hours to master. Because a new tile spawns after every swipe, the board constantly fills up, and one careless move can trap your high-value tiles behind a wall of small numbers. The game ends when the grid is full and no merges remain — so every swipe matters.

Originally created by Gabriele Cirulli in 2014 as an open-source project, 2048 became one of the most viral browser games ever made, played by hundreds of millions of people worldwide. Its clean math, quick rounds, and "just one more try" feeling make it perfect for a short break at school, at work, or on your phone. This version runs entirely in your browser: there is nothing to install, no account to create, and it works on any modern device.

How to Play

1 · SlideUse arrow keys (or swipe on mobile) to move every tile in one direction.
2 · MergeWhen two tiles with the same number touch, they combine into their sum: 2+2=4, 4+4=8, and so on.
3 · SurviveA new 2 or 4 tile spawns after every move. Don't let the grid fill up.
4 · WinCreate the 2048 tile to win — then keep going for a high score if you dare.

Tips & Strategy

  • Pick a corner and defend it. Keep your biggest tile locked in one corner and never let it leave.
  • Build a snake. Arrange your tiles in descending order along an edge, like 256 → 128 → 64 → 32.
  • Use only three directions. If your corner is bottom-left, avoid pressing "up" — it frees your anchor tile.
  • Don't chase small merges. Think two moves ahead; merging small tiles at the wrong time breaks your chain.
  • Stay calm when the board fills. Look for the move that opens the most space, not the biggest merge.
